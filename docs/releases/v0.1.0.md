The first release: an endless block world you can dig, build in and get lost in, running on
the handheld itself. Everything about **staying alive** in it came later, in
[v0.2.0](v0.2.0.html).

## The world

[More →](../world/index.html)

- An **endless streaming world**, 64 blocks tall, generated from a seed you can type in.
- **Five biomes** — plains, forest, birch wood, mountains and sand flats — chosen from slow
  temperature and humidity fields, with the ground blending smoothly between them and the
  surface changing at a line.
- **Caves**: long tunnels that get wider the deeper they go, with a few places where one breaks
  the surface and opens wide enough to walk into.
- **Ore veins** rather than scattered blocks — coal to y = 40, iron to y = 28 — so finding one
  block is a reason to dig sideways.
- Trees, flowers, tall grass, cactus, snow on the high ground, sandstone under the desert.
- **The Far Lands.** Walk far enough west and the ground becomes Minecraft Beta 1.7.3's own
  terrain generator, fed coordinates past the point where its arithmetic overflowed: a wall of
  stone from the bedrock to the sky, riddled with tunnels that all run west. Not an imitation —
  Beta's generator, ported, breaking the same way. There are signs along the last ordinary land
  before it.
- **Day and night**, with a sun, a moon, clouds and stars, and light that changes as the day
  goes.
- **Block and sky lighting**, and torches you can hang on walls.
- **Water you can swim in**, with a proper swimming stroke and a fall that the water kills.

## Playing

- **Walking, jumping, sneaking, swimming**, and a step-up so you climb one block but never two.
- **Breaking and placing** any block, with a crosshair and a highlight on what you are aiming
  at.
- **Tree felling**: one swing on a trunk brings the whole tree down.
- **Sand and gravel fall** when you dig out from under them.
- **Fred** — a first-person arm that holds what you are holding, and a third-person view.
- A **position overlay**, and **screenshots** onto the SD card.

## Items and crafting

[More →](../recipes.html)

- **Inventory and a six-slot hotbar**, with item icons and stack counts.
- **Tools** in three tiers — wooden, stone and iron — with **durability**, and a speed bonus
  only on their own class of block.
- **A recipe book instead of a crafting grid.** No shapes to remember: you pick what you want
  from a searchable list, it shows you what is missing, and it **crafts the intermediate steps
  for you** — ask for a pickaxe holding nothing but logs and it makes the planks and the sticks
  on the way. All or nothing, so a failed plan gives your materials back untouched.
- The **crafting table**, and the four things you can make without one.
- The **furnace**: iron ore into ingots, sand into glass, cobblestone into stone, and logs into
  coal. Anything wooden burns as fuel, including spent wooden tools.
- **Chests** — 27 stacks, and they give everything back when broken.
- The **trash chest**, which destroys what you put in it ten minutes later, so you can change
  your mind.
- The **disassembly bench**, which takes most crafted things apart and gives the materials back
  — not in Minecraft, and the reason iron spent on the wrong thing is not iron lost.

## Settings

- **Every control rebindable**, from a controls menu.
- **Gyroscope look** — turn the badge and the view turns with it, alongside the arrow keys.
- Graphics settings: textures on or off, render scale, view distance.
- Audio and display settings.
- **32 languages.**

## Under the hood

Not visible, but this is where most of the work went:

- A **software rasteriser** with a banded z-buffer held in internal memory, running on both
  cores, drawing to the display's own buffers by page flip.
- **Chunk generation and meshing on the second core**, so the world streams in without the
  frame rate noticing.
- **Worlds on the SD card** with a region format, an index, and saves that happen when a chunk
  unloads, when you ask, and when you quit — never every tick.
- **Replay recording**, and a deterministic tick: every random number in the game is a hash of
  where and when, so a replay reproduces a world exactly.
- **The player's data moved out of the app's install directory**, so updating the game can never
  delete a world.
