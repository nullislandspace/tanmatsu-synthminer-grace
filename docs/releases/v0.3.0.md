**Nothing is hunting you on the hillside any more.** Hostile mobs are gone from the
overworld. They live in a second dimension — the **Deep Mine** — which you reach
through a **mine shaft** you build for sixteen iron ingots.

Worlds from v0.1.0, v0.2.0 and v0.2.1 open unchanged. 32 languages.

## The Deep Mine

[More →](../world/deep-mine.html)

A second dimension **inside the same world save** — not a second world. One seed, one
save file, one player, one inventory. What is not shared is the terrain, the
creatures, the light and the sky.

- **One big cave with a sealed bedrock roof**, and nothing nether about it: normal
  stone, ore and lava, exactly the kind you already dig through upstairs.
- **Rough on a large scale.** Hills, terraces and cliffs you have to walk around —
  43.6% of the terrace edges are a two-block step or more, and a two-block *climb*
  is a wall while a two-block *drop* is a jump.
- **Claustrophobic in places.** 35.5% of the cave has 18 or more blocks of headroom,
  15.4% has one to four, and 1.8% is solid rock you dig through.
- **Four districts**: a big Cavern, Hills, a Lava Sea, and a Low Hall where the roof
  lands on the floor.
- **No sun and no moon.** The light is a constant, so a torch is for *seeing* rather
  than for keeping anything away.
- **Iron is three times denser** than upstairs — 2.4% of the rock against the
  overworld's 0.8% on the same sample.
- **Diamond exists here and nowhere else**: 0.06% of the rock, in clusters.

## The mine shaft

[More →](../devices/mine-shaft.html)

**16 iron ingots at a crafting table**, one item, sixteen blocks: four posts on the
corners of a 3×3 footprint, three high, joined by four braces at the top. Stand in
the open centre and you cross to the same column in the other world.

- **1:1 in x and z** — not Minecraft's 1:8. The shaft you stand in and the shaft you
  arrive in are in the same column, so the way back always exists.
- **The height is different at each end**, because each end stands on its own ground.
- **The generator never makes one.** Every shaft in either world is one somebody
  placed, or the far end of a column somebody crossed.
- The far end is built the first time you cross to a column, and it levels the ground
  it stands on first.

## Zombies and skeletons

[More →](../mobs/index.html)

The first things in the game that fight back, and the only ones.

- **Generated with the terrain, once.** About one chunk in eight holds a pack of two
  or three. **They do not come back**: a stretch you clear stays clear.
- **They come after you** from 16 blocks, walk to within 1.7 and swing once a second —
  3 health from a zombie, 2 from a skeleton.
- **They hurt each other too.** If a zombie's swing lands on a skeleton, the skeleton
  is now after the zombie.
- **They drop nothing.** That is deliberate: a mob is a hazard, not a resource, and
  nothing in the game is only obtainable from one.

## Lava

[More →](../world/lava.html)

- **It flows**, like water, at a third of the speed and for three blocks. A lake
  breached with a pickaxe runs out of the hole.
- **It burns whoever is standing in it** — you or a zombie, at the same rate: 2
  health every half second. Five seconds from full health to dead.
- **Water meeting lava makes stone.** Both of them stop being fluids.

## Dying down there

There is **one spawn point and it is the overworld's**. A bed cannot be placed in the
Deep Mine, and dying there carries you back upstairs to your bed or your original
spawn — with half your health and your whole inventory, exactly as dying anywhere
else does.

## Also new

- **An optional external VFD display.** If your Tanmatsu has one fitted, it shows the
  name of the item in your hand while you are in a world, blank for an empty hand, and
  **SynthMiner** in the menus. It is powered down when the app exits. On a badge
  without a tube it does nothing at all.
- **Saving a world is a screen with a bar that fills.** The chunks go out a handful a
  frame instead of all in one block, and if the card refuses a chunk the screen says
  how many rather than hanging.
- **Beds are refused in the Deep Mine**, with a line saying why.
- **A wall is drawn by the chunk that owns the solid cell**, which fixes the invisible
  walls and the blocks that were there but not drawn.
- **The Deep Mine's terrain was rebuilt once**, after the first version looked like an
  unfinished overworld: the roof is now a distance above the floor rather than a second
  surface, the terraces make cliffs, and the lava is a level rather than scattered
  blobs.

## Fixed

- **Shafts appearing in the Deep Mine that nobody placed.** The generator used to stamp
  one per grid square; it does not any more.
- **Arriving on top of the Deep Mine instead of inside its shaft** — the arrival cell is
  now the centre of the frame, and the frame is built before you are put in it.
- **Mobs drawn as a few small floating squares.** Both kinds were drawn with the
  four-legged animal's shape, which gave a 1.8-tall humanoid a body of no length. They
  are upright figures now: torso, two legs, two arms, head.
- **A crossing that refused to happen.** The switch now waits for the card to finish its
  own writes instead of giving up, which is what "the shaft would not open" was.
- **Dying in the Deep Mine did nothing** beyond resetting your health to half.
- **Lava standing in mid-air**, unconnected to anything. It is a level now, so every
  lake has a shore and a basin.
- **Water and lava each ignoring the other.**
- **Chunk saves that could not be guaranteed to fit**, and SD write failures that left
  the game stuck on the saving screen.
- **A title screen that would not come back** after quitting out of the Deep Mine.
- **A crash when quitting a world, and the world that read as "(damaged)" afterwards.** The
  chunks were all there — the small file holding the world's name, your position and your
  spawn was being rewritten in place, and a crash half way through left it empty. It is
  written somewhere else and moved into place now, and a half-written one is recovered
  before the game decides whether a slot holds a world at all.
- **A chunk the card would not take was anonymous.** Every chunk load and save failure now
  names the chunk it happened to, in the game's own error log.
