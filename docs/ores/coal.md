---
title: Coal ore
summary: The common one. Fuel, torches, and the first thing you should stockpile.
---

| | |
|---|---|
| **Found** | Anywhere below **y = 40** |
| **Needs** | Any pickaxe — wooden is enough |
| **Drops** | 1 **coal** (not the block) |
| **Vein** | Common: about a third of the coarse grid cells carry one, roughly 2 blocks across |
| **Break time** | 10 seconds by hand *(and you get nothing)*, 5 s wooden, 2.5 s stone, 1.7 s iron |

## Where to look

y = 40 is high — well up the side of a hill, not deep. In practice you find coal
almost as soon as you start digging, and a mountainside often has it exposed.

You do not need to go on an expedition for coal. You need to **not run out of it**,
which is a different problem: torches, the furnace and the stove all eat it.

## What it is for

| Use | How much |
|---|---|
| **Torches** | 1 coal + 1 stick → **4 torches** (by hand, no table needed) |
| **Furnace fuel** | 1600 ticks — **eight** items smelted per coal |
| **Stove fuel** | The same 1600 ticks, which is a little over one dish |
| **Trash chest** | 8 planks + 1 coal |
| **Disassembly bench** | 4 planks + 1 coal |

Coal is by far the best fuel in the game. For comparison, a log or a plank burns
for 300 ticks and a stick for 100 — so one coal is worth more than five logs, and
burning logs for fuel is something you do when you have no coal rather than a
plan.

## Coal without a mine

A furnace turns **1 log into 1 coal**. This is a genuine conversion and not a
trick: if you have trees and one coal to start the furnace, you have coal.

It is not efficient — a log burned as fuel gives 300 ticks and a log smelted into
coal gives you 1600 — which is exactly why it is worth doing. Burn your worst wood
to convert your good wood into coal.
