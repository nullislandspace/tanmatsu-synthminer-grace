## Animals are generated with the land

This is the most important rule about them, and it is not Minecraft's.

A herd is placed **when a chunk is first generated**, and nothing ever spawns
later. Animals do not appear at night, they do not appear when you walk away and
come back, and a field you have emptied stays empty.

What follows from that:

- **Breeding is the supply.** Kill the last two pigs in reach and you are walking
  to the next unexplored valley for another. Build a pen early.
- **Animals stay where you put them.** They are saved with the chunk they are
  standing in, so a cow in a field is in that field tomorrow, and a herd in a chunk
  you have not visited costs the game nothing at all.
- **Nothing wanders off the edge of the world.** An unloaded chunk is solid to an
  animal exactly as it is to you, so they cannot be lost by walking away.
- Roughly **one chunk in 25** of a suitable biome holds a herd of a given kind, of
  two to four animals.

## Feeding and breeding

Hold the animal's food and press <kbd>E</kbd> on it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **An adult** goes *in the mood* for **30 seconds** |
| **Two in the mood** within **2.5 blocks** of each other produce a **calf**, and both then rest for **5 minutes** |
| **A fed animal walks towards** another fed one of its kind up to **16 blocks** away — you do not have to herd them together |
| **A fed animal ignores food you are waving.** It has somewhere else to be. This is why a stack of potatoes lasts |
| **A calf** grows up in **10 minutes of playing**, and every feed takes a tenth off that |

<p class="note"><b>Feed two, then leave them alone.</b> The commonest mistake is
feeding one animal five times. It eats the first potato, goes in the mood, and then
refuses the rest — the food stays in your hand, which is the game telling you to go
and feed a <em>different</em> one.</p>

A baby follows whoever is holding its food, which is how you move a young animal
somewhere. So does an adult, within **7 blocks** — unless it has already been fed.

## Penning them in

[**Fences and gates**](../devices/fences.html) are what keep a herd where you want
it. A fence is a block animals will not walk through and you cannot jump over; a
gate opens for you and stays shut for them.

Two animals and a pen is a permanent supply of everything they give. This is worth
doing on your first day in a place you intend to stay.

## Killing them

Hit with <kbd>Q</kbd>. Animals flinch and run from the blow, and are briefly
unhittable after each one.

| In hand | Damage | Pig or cow (10) | Sheep or dog (8) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fist, hoe, shears | 1 | 10 hits | 8 hits |
| Wooden axe | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Stone axe | 4 | 3 | 2 |
| Iron axe | 5 | 2 | 2 |
| Wooden / stone / iron pickaxe or shovel | 2 / 3 / 4 | 5 / 4 / 3 | 4 / 3 / 2 |

An **axe** is the weapon in this game. There is no sword.

<p class="note"><b>A dog drops nothing.</b> Killing one pays exactly zero — it is
not livestock. Don't.</p>

## What they do to you

Animals **shove**. Two cows in a doorway push each other apart, and a cow walking
into you moves you both — you take about a third of it. It is a soft push rather
than a wall, so nothing ever gets wedged through a solid block, but a herd
crowding a gateway will move you out of it.
