Everything hollow below **y = 24** is full of water. Above that, water appears
wherever you put it — and then it goes where water goes.

## Sources and flow

There are two kinds of water cell, and the difference matters:

- A **source** is where water comes *from*. Every cell of the sea is one, and so
  is a cell you emptied a bucket into. A source never dries up on its own.
- **Flow** is what a source produces. It spreads sideways a few blocks, thinning
  as it goes, and falls straight down as far as there is room. Take the source
  away and the flow it was feeding drains.

You can see the difference: flowing water is **visibly shallower** than a source,
and a falling column is drawn as a waterfall.

<p class="note"><b>Two sources make a third.</b> A cell with a source on two
sides of it, standing on something solid, becomes a source itself. Dig a two-block
trench, put a bucket of water in each end, and the middle is an infinite spring —
you can fill a bucket from it for ever. This is the standard way to get water to a
field that is nowhere near a lake.</p>

## Water and you

- You **swim** rather than sink: hold <kbd>Space</kbd> to rise, and you move at
  about half walking speed.
- **Water cancels a fall completely.** Any drop, from any height, costs nothing if
  there is water at the bottom. A pool at the foot of a cliff is a lift.
- Swimming costs about half again as much hunger per block as walking. It is the
  expensive way to travel.
- You can see through it. Looking down into a lake from the surface shows you the
  bottom, which is how you find the sand that rice grows on.

## The bucket

An [iron bucket](../tools/bucket.html) is the only thing that moves water about.

- **Use it on a source** to fill it. Only on a source — dipping it in flow gets
  you nothing, because flow is not water that is *there*, it is water that is
  *going past*.
- **Use a full one on a block face** to put a source down.
- A full bucket does not stack, and neither does an empty one.

## What water is for

| | |
|---|---|
| **Farming** | Tilled soil is wet or dry depending on whether there is water within **4 blocks** of it on the same level, and dry soil refuses seeds. One source can water a 9×9 patch. |
| **Rice** | Planted *in* the water, not beside it: exactly one block deep, on sand. |
| **Fishing** | Point a baited rod at any water in reach. |
| **Getting down safely** | See above. |
| **Cows** | A bucket of milk is one use of a bucket on a cow, and milk is both food and the only way to make cheese. |

<p class="note"><b>The water rule for crops is only checked twice</b> — when you
till the soil, and when you offer it a seed. Nothing watches a field while it
grows, so draining the moat afterwards will not kill the crop. It will stop you
planting the next one.</p>
