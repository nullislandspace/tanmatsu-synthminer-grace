title: The Deep Mine
summary: The second dimension — one sealed cave, richer iron, diamond, lava, and the only place anything hunts you.

The Deep Mine is a **second dimension inside the same world save**. Not a second
world: one seed, one save, one player, one inventory, one compass. What is not shared
is the terrain, the creatures, the light and the sky.

You get there through a [mine shaft](../devices/mine-shaft.html).

<p class="note"><b>It is not the Nether.</b> The comparison is about the <em>shape</em>
of the place — a sealed ceiling, no sky, essentially one cave — and not about the
material. There is no nether stone, no nether wart, no glowstone and no obsidian. It is
the same stone you already dig upstairs, with lava in the low ground.</p>

## What it looks like

| | |
|---|---|
| **The floor** | Roughly y 10 to 22, rolling in hills and terraces |
| **The roof** | Bedrock, roughly y 18 to 41, sealed on every column |
| **The lava** | One level for the whole dimension, at **y 12** |
| **The light** | A constant. No sun, no moon, no day and no night |
| **The height of the cave** | From nothing at all to 22 blocks of headroom |

The roof is not a second surface wandering above the ground — it is a **distance**
above it, and what varies as you walk is that distance. That is what gives the cave a
shape: a hall, then a squeeze, then a wall of solid rock where the roof has come down
onto the floor.

**It is rough on a large scale and smooth on a small one.** The ground comes in
terraces, and 43.6% of the steps between them are two blocks or more. Going *down* two
blocks is a jump off a ledge; going *up* two blocks is not possible, so a terrace edge
is a cliff you walk around. Measured over the whole cave, **95.8% of it is reachable on
foot from any one spot** — the rest is rock you dig through, which is what a mine is
for.

## How open it is

| Headroom | Share of the cave |
|---|---|
| **18 blocks or more** — a hall | 35.5% |
| 5–17 blocks | 47.3% |
| **1–4 blocks** — a squeeze you crouch through | 15.4% |
| None — solid rock | 1.8% |

## The four districts

They are chosen from slow fields of their own, so a district is a few minutes across on
foot rather than something that flickers block by block.

| District | What it is | Iron | Diamond |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cavern** | The default, and the biggest room — up to 24 blocks of headroom, though the same district also holds squeezes | Rich | Occasional |
| **Hills** | The rough one: real slopes, real drops, and a ceiling high enough that you can see the slope you are standing on | Rich | Occasional |
| **Lava Sea** | Low ground with a lake on it, and shores where the ground rises out of it | **Richest** | **Most likely** |
| **Low Hall** | The claustrophobic one: the roof lands on the floor, and the cave becomes walls you have to dig through | Rich | Rare |

Over a large sample: Cavern 40–58%, Low Hall 20–38%, Hills 6–24%, Lava Sea 6–13%.

## Lava

The lava is **one level for the whole dimension**, exactly as the sea upstairs is one
level. That is why it behaves: every column whose ground lies below it is filled up to
it, so the surface comes out flat, the shore comes out where the ground rises, and each
lake is walled by its own basin. About **3–7% of the cave floor is lava**.

See [Lava](lava.html) — it flows, and it hurts.

## Ores

| Ore | Here | In the overworld |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | **2.4% of the rock** | 0.8% |
| **Coal** | Common, and richer in the Low Hall | Common up to y 40 |
| **Diamond** | **0.06%, in clusters** | Does not exist |

Iron in the Deep Mine is the same ore with the same tool requirement — a **stone
pickaxe or better** — and it appears at a height you can reach without digging down,
because the cave floor *is* the depth.

## Light, and why torches still matter

There is no sky, so the game lights the cave with a constant — **12 of the 15 levels of
light**, enough to see the cave by and not enough to see it clearly. **Torches do not keep
anything away** — nothing in this game spawns by darkness — but a torch's own light is
**14**, so it still makes one patch of a corridor brighter than the rest of it, and it is
still the trail you follow back out.

There is **no day and no night** down there. The world's clock keeps running, so crops,
furnaces and composters elsewhere carry on, but nothing down here changes with it, and
sleeping is not a thing you can do.

## One world at a time

Only one dimension is loaded at a time. Crossing over saves everything you have
changed, releases the whole loaded world, and streams in the other one around the
shaft you arrive at — which is why there is a short **Crossing over** screen rather
than an instant change.

<p class="note"><b>Your things carry across.</b> Health, hunger, the inventory, the
recipe book and the compass are the same in both dimensions. Only the world is
different.</p>

## What is not there

- **No animals.** Pigs, cows, sheep and dogs are overworld creatures. Down there it is
  [zombies and skeletons](../mobs/index.html).
- **No bed.** There is no night to skip, and the game has one spawn point — the
  overworld's. See [Bed](../devices/bed.html).
- **No trees, no grass, no water on the surface.** Water exists where you bring it, and
  where it meets lava it turns both into stone.
- **No Far Lands.** The wall at the west edge of the world is the overworld's.
