---
title: Trees and saplings
summary: Felling a whole tree with one swing, and why wood is renewable now.
---

There are **two kinds of tree**: oak, which is everywhere, and birch, which grows
in rare birch woods and has a white trunk.

## Felling

Point at any part of a tree's **trunk** and break it. The **whole tree comes down
at once** — trunk, branches and leaves — and every log of it drops where you are
standing. You do not have to climb it and you do not have to chop it block by
block.

An [axe](../tools/axe.html) makes it quicker: a log is 2 seconds by hand, 1 with a
wooden axe, half a second with a stone one.

<p class="note"><b>This only works on trees the world grew.</b> Logs you placed
yourself are not part of a tree and come down one at a time — so a log cabin does
not collapse when you take a wall out.</p>

## Saplings

Felling a tree drops **one or two saplings**, of whatever species you just cut
down. That is per **tree**, not per log — one oak gives you one or two, not forty.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Plant on** | **Grass or dirt** — *not* tilled soil, and it does not need water |
| **Sapling to tree** | **1 in-game day** — 20 minutes of playing, or one night's sleep |
| **Needs** | Room above it: a sapling with a ceiling two blocks over its head will not grow |
| **Grows into** | The same species you felled — a birch sapling grows a birch |

Point at the grass and press <kbd>E</kbd>. The sapling goes in the cell above.

A grown tree is the same tree the world would have put there: same shape, same
height range, and it can be felled with one swing like any other.

<p class="note"><b>A sapling with no headroom is not lost.</b> If the tree will not
fit when the sapling comes of age, nothing happens and it tries again later — so a
sapling under a ledge will grow the day you take the ledge away.</p>

## Why this matters

**There is no recipe anywhere that makes a log.** Before saplings, a player who
cleared the forest around their house had cleared it for good. Stone, ore, crops,
wool, meat and milk all come back; wood did not.

Now it does — which also means you can plant an orchard where you want one instead
of walking to the nearest wood every time you need planks.

Two saplings per tree is break-even at worst and profit at best, so a woodland you
replant never runs down.

## Leaves

Leaves drop nothing when you break them, and they come down with the tree anyway.
[Shears](../tools/shears.html) cut them faster than anything else, which is the
only reason to bother.

What leaves *are* good for is **compost**: they rot down in a
[composter](../devices/composter.html), and so do flowers, tall grass and crop
stalks. A felled tree's worth of leaves is a useful amount of fertiliser and
fishing bait for something you were clearing anyway.

## What wood is for

Nearly everything early. One log is four planks; two planks are four sticks.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Planks** | The crafting table (4), chest (8), trash chest (8), composter (7), cheese maker (7), the disassembly bench (4), fences, the bed (3), and the stove's chest (8) |
| **Sticks** | Every tool wants two. The fishing rod wants three |
| **Fuel** | A log or a plank burns for 300 ticks, a stick for 100 — against 1600 for coal |
| **Coal** | 1 log smelted in a furnace → 1 coal |
