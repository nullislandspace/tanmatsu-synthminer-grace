## Finding the first one

Wild tomato plants grow **in birch woods**. Within a birch wood they are the
commonest of the three wild crops — but birch woods are themselves rare, so a
tomato is in practice the hardest of the three to come across.

A birch wood is unmistakable: white trunks, close together, pale green leaves.

## The one that needs a table

A tomato is not a seed. Take it to a **crafting table** and turn it into **two
tomato seeds**, and one fruit becomes two plants. That is why a single wild tomato
is enough to start a farm rather than being a thing you ate once.

It does not work backwards — two seeds will not become a tomato again.

## What it is for

| Use | Needs |
|---|---|
| **Eating it raw** — 1 hunger, no saturation | Just the tomato |
| **Grilled tomatoes** — 2 hunger, no saturation | 1 tomato, on a [kitchen stove](../devices/kitchen-stove.html) |
| **Kebab** — 8 hunger, 7 saturation | 1 tomato + mutton + beans + 2 wheat |
| **Tomato seeds** | 1 tomato at a table → 2 seeds |

<p class="note"><b>The only raw food in the game besides milk.</b> Everything else
— pork, beef, mutton, fish, wheat, rice, beans, potatoes — is an ingredient until a
stove has been at it. A tomato is what you eat on the first day, and cooking one
doubles what it is worth for no ingredients at all.</p>

With the best yield of any crop (2–4) and the short one-day cycle, a tomato patch
is the fastest food per plot in the game. It is also the least *nourishing* per
bite, because grilled tomatoes carry no saturation — fine at home, poor for
travelling.
