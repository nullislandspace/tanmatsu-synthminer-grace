## Wool

Hold shears and press <kbd>E</kbd> on an adult [sheep](../animals/sheep.html). You get
**1 to 3 wool**, and the shears wear by one.

The sheep is left **shorn** and gives nothing more until its fleece grows back, which
takes **twenty minutes of playing** — and, unlike a composter, that does *not* happen while
you sleep or while the sheep is in a chunk you have walked away from. A shorn sheep looks
shorn, so you can tell at a glance which of a flock is ready.

Lambs cannot be sheared.

<p class="note"><b>This is what makes a flock worth keeping.</b> Killing a sheep gives
you 1–2 mutton and no more sheep. Shearing one gives you wool every day for ever. Two
sheep and a fence is a permanent wool supply, and 220 uses is 220 shearings — far
more than any flock will get through.</p>

## What wool is for

| | |
|---|---|
| [**Bed**](../devices/bed.html) | 3 wool + 3 planks |
| **String** | 1 wool → 3 string, at a crafting table |
| [**Fishing rod**](fishing-rod.html) | 3 sticks + 2 string |
| **Compost** | Wool is the only animal product that rots down |

So the chain is: **iron → shears → wool → a bed, and string → a rod.** Both of the
things that make the mid-game pleasant are behind two iron ingots.

## Leaves

Shears cut leaves faster than anything else — 0.2 seconds against 0.4 by hand. Leaves
drop nothing either way, and a felled tree brings its own leaves down with it, so this
is a minor use.

It is worth knowing if you want a lot of leaves for the
[composter](../devices/composter.html), which is a genuine reason to clear a canopy.

## Worth making early

Two ingots is cheap — the same as an iron hoe and less than a bucket. Of everything
iron makes, shears are the earliest one that changes what you can do.
