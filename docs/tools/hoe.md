| **Recipe** | 2 planks + 2 sticks | 2 cobblestone + 2 sticks | 2 iron ingots + 2 sticks |

Made at a [crafting table](../devices/crafting-table.html).

## The one thing it does

Point at **grass or dirt with nothing on top of it** and press <kbd>E</kbd>. It
becomes **farmland**, and farmland is the only thing four of the five crops will grow
on.

The farmland comes out **wet** or **dry** depending on whether there is water within
**4 blocks** of it on the same level. **Dry farmland refuses seeds** — it tells you
the ground is too dry rather than taking the seed and never growing anything.

So the working order is: find or make water, *then* till.

See [Plants and farming](../plants/index.html).

## A hoe never digs faster

This is the thing to understand about hoes, and it is unusual: **a hoe does not speed
up any block in the game.** Not grass, not dirt, not crops. Swinging a hoe at
anything is exactly as fast as swinging your fist.

Which means the three tiers buy you **nothing but uses**:

| | Plots tilled |
|---|---|
| Wooden hoe | 60 |
| Stone hoe | 130 |
| Iron hoe | 250 |

A wooden hoe is two planks and two sticks and will till a field of sixty plots, which
is a bigger farm than most people build. **Make a wooden one.** An iron hoe is not a
better hoe; it is a hoe you stop having to re-make.

## It is not a weapon

A hoe does 1 damage, the same as your bare hand. Swinging a hoe at a cow is not a
plan.

## Breaking farmland

A shovel takes farmland back to plain dirt, and so do your hands. Breaking a plot
does not destroy the crop standing on it — but the crop will be sitting on dirt and
cannot be replanted until you till it again.
