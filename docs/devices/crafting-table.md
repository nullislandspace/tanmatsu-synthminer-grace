## There is no grid

SynthMiner has no 3×3 crafting square and no shapes to remember. Press <kbd>C</kbd> and
you get a **recipe book**: a searchable list of things you can make, each one showing
what it needs and how many you can make right now.

Pick one, and it is made. That is the whole interface.

The list is filtered to what you have **seen** — a recipe shows up once you have held at
least one of its ingredients — so the book grows as you play instead of listing the
whole game at you on the first morning.

## Made with bare hands

Four recipes need no table at all. Open the book anywhere:

| Make | From |
|---|---|
| **4 planks** | 1 log |
| **4 sticks** | 2 planks |
| **4 torches** | 1 coal + 1 stick |
| **A crafting table** | 4 planks |

That is deliberately the shortest possible list: finding wood is enough to get started,
and everything else waits for a table.

## Everything else

Stand next to a table — or point at it and press <kbd>E</kbd> — and the book opens with
the rest of the game in it: all the tools, the furnace, chests, the composter, fences,
the bed, the makers, the stove, the fishing rod, tomato seeds, and string.

See [Every recipe](../recipes.html) for the complete list.

## It crafts the steps for you

This is the thing worth knowing about the book.

Ask for a **wooden pickaxe** while carrying nothing but logs, and it will turn a log
into planks and some of those planks into sticks and then make the pickaxe. You do not
have to work out the chain.

<p class="note"><b>It is all or nothing.</b> If anything in the chain turns out to be
missing, the whole plan is abandoned and your materials are handed back exactly as they
were. You will never end up holding planks you did not want because a craft got half
way.</p>

It only does this for things that can be made **by hand or at a table**. It will not
start a furnace on your behalf, or a sausage maker, or a stove — those take fuel and
time, and neither is something a menu should decide for you.

## Undoing a craft

The [disassembly bench](disassembly-bench.html) is the other half of this. It takes most
crafted things apart and gives the materials back, which is what makes experimenting
cheap.
